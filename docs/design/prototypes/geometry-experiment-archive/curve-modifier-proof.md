## 1. Executive Summary

This technology proof evaluates the official Three.js `CurveModifier.Flow` addon (`examples/jsm/modifiers/CurveModifier.js`, pinned to Three.js `0.160.0`) as a candidate for constructing the sculptural 3D fluid mesh background in the Hero section, following the visual rejection of previous custom spine-extrusion experiments.

**Verdict: ABANDON `CurveModifier.Flow` for the Hero Fluid Mesh.**

While `CurveModifier.Flow` integrates cleanly into our zero-build Three.js r160 static architecture with zero console errors and 60 FPS GPU performance, it exhibits severe mathematical and geometric limitations:
1. It relies strictly on standard **Frenet-Serret frames** rather than **Rotation-Minimizing Frames (RMF)**, causing rigid orientation locks dictated solely by spine curvature.
2. It lacks native banking, twist, or roll control along the spine.
3. Extruding a 2D cross-section along a 1D spatial spine fundamentally produces a bent aerodynamic wing/canopy rather than the multi-layered, self-occluding, organic fluid wave seen in the primary reference (`hero-fluid-mesh-primary.jpg`).

## 4. In-Depth Technical & Geometric Analysis

### 4.1. The Frenet Frame Flaw
Under the hood, `CurveModifier.Flow` samples curve points and generates Frenet frames using Three.js's `Curve.computeFrenetFrames()`:
$$\mathbf{T} = \frac{\mathbf{r}'(s)}{\|\mathbf{r}'(s)\|}, \quad \mathbf{N} = \frac{\mathbf{T}'(s)}{\|\mathbf{T}'(s)\|}, \quad \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{T} \times \mathbf{N}$$

In the vertex shader, local mesh coordinates $(x, y, z)$ are displaced:
$$\mathbf{P}_{\text{deformed}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{curve}}(s) + y \cdot \mathbf{N}(s) + z \cdot \mathbf{B}(s)$$

This introduces three critical issues:
1. **Curvature-Locked Orientation:** The normal $\mathbf{N}$ points toward the center of curvature. If a curve straightens out ($\kappa \to 0$) or bends in 3D, the normal spins unexpectedly.
2. **Absence of Parallel Transport (RMF):** Unlike the Double Reflection RMF used in our Blender Python script, Frenet frames do not minimize rotation along the curve, producing unnatural twisting when the spine sweeps through 3D space.
3. **No Roll/Banking Uniform:** The shader lacks a rotation angle parameter $\theta(s)$ around the tangent $\mathbf{T}$. To orient the profile relative to the camera, the input mesh had to be rotated globally (`baseGeometry.rotateX(-Math.PI / 2)`), which applies rigidly across all vertices.

## 5. Architectural Verdict & Recommendations

### Why `CurveModifier.Flow` Must Be Abandoned
1. **Wrong Topological Paradigm:** The visual authority (`hero-fluid-mesh-primary.jpg`) is **not** an extruded tube or sweep. It is a complex multi-fold sculptural surface featuring independent foreground crests, midground cascades, and background flow fields. Forcing this form into a single spine extrusion (whether in Blender or Three.js) guarantees an artificial "bent ribbon/wing" aesthetic.
2. **GPU Texture Modification Overhead:** Adding per-point banking, non-uniform scaling, or twist to `CurveModifier` would require monkey-patching the addon's internal shader chunks and writing a custom data-texture encoder. Even with those additions, the core topology would remain a single extruded ribbon.

### Recommended Next Steps
1. **Retire Spine Extrusion as the Primary Fluid Strategy:** Conclude definitively that sweeping a 1D spline with a 2D cross-section cannot reproduce the reference.
2. **Evaluate Multi-Layer Procedural Heightfield / Curvature Surface:** Explore generating the surface via multi-frequency displaced parametric grids or coupled Bézier patches with genuine self-occlusion, calibrated to match the reference's composition.
3. **Preserve Proof Artifacts:** Keep `docs/design/prototypes/curve-modifier-proof/` as a permanent reference benchmark for why Three.js curve deformation was evaluated and discarded.
